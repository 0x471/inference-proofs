    Our notes (1)

    We apply the test FlashProve's row meets: we read a petal as transparent when a sentence among its quotes says what soundness rests on and none of it is a setup that produced a secret (GL-10, amended 2026-11-15). SASH states its soundness as stwo's conjectured accounting over the FRI parameters (util.rs L9-L12). It says the setup's two trees are a shape tree the verifier derives from the public parameters and a model tree compared with the published model commitment (prover/README.md L117-L119, docs/statement.md L127-L129). The Fixed statement it calls trusted by the verifier is those parameters and roots (compose.rs L7-L11). No sentence says in words that no setup produced a secret, so we read the petal as transparent and grade it Mostly met at most, as FlashProve's is.

    No test of ours bears on it

    Why this grade
    Met needs, in full

    The publisher's own sentence that checking needs no secret setup and no trusted party.

    What it has

    The publisher states that the verifier takes two byte strings and the proof, and nothing else. We read this sentence to mean that anyone can check a proof, but the publisher does not say so in words. (the publisher's words: “The verifier (verify_composed) takes those two byte strings and the serialized proof, and nothing else” (new tab))

    The publisher states its soundness as stwo's conjectured estimate (see Integrity). Its setup commits two trees: a shape tree, whose root the verifier builds itself from the public settings, and a model tree, whose root the verifier compares with the published commitment. It calls some values Fixed, “produced at setup, trusted by the verifier”. It lists them as the model's shape and the settings of its circuits, the security settings, the trees' column sizes, and the pinned root of the tree that holds every table, weight and scale column. We read “trusted” here to mean that the verifier takes these values as given. These are settings and a tree root, not the weights themselves, and the publisher calls none of them a secret.

    So, on our reading, soundness rests on cryptographic assumptions only, and no setup step produced a secret. But the publisher does not say this in one sentence.
