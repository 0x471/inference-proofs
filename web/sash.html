    Glyph

    Five properties, each graded on its own. The glyph at the top of this page draws them as five petals. Grades, from best to worst: Met, Mostly met, Partly met. Below those come three grades drawn red, not ranked against each other: Not met (the publisher states something that rules the property out, or a limit too weak to count); Not stated (the documents we read say nothing, and a guarantee nobody states is one nobody can rely on); Failed (one of our tests failed). Not decided means our part is not done, so there is no grade yet.

    Beside each grade, what it comes from: our test; our rebuild (we run the publisher's proof through the proof-checking program ourselves); the publisher's words; or our reading, with the number of points it covers (no single sentence of the publisher's states the point, so we read it from several passages, which are linked). A grade that uses our reading can reach Mostly met, not Met: Met needs the publisher's own words.

    Words used below. The publisher: the team that makes the system. A proof: the file a system sends with each reply. A verifier: the program that checks a proof. A commitment: a short value the publisher computes from the model's weights and publishes; every proof is checked against it. A theorem: a mathematical result the publisher proves. A chance written 2^-128 is 1 in 2^128; the larger the exponent, the smaller the chance.

Proving usually runs a copy whose numbers are stored in fewer bits (quantization), and SASH proves W8A8 (8-bit weights and activations), 8 bits per weight for part of the weights, computed with integer weights and floating-point activations. Each row below is fed the same 15 prompts, each followed by the original's reply. Its Fidelity figure is the share of the reply's positions where its top token is the original's next token: 100 % means the original's choice at every position. The 8-bit copy (Q8_0) and the 4-bit copy (Q4_K_M) are the original with its weights rounded to 8 bits, and mostly to 4 bits, by llama.cpp; we run them without a proof, as yardsticks. The level file fixes, before any run, which copies are honest (the original run again and its 8-bit and 4-bit copies) and which are damaged on purpose; the bar is the highest-scoring damaged copy that every honest one scores above.

    Why top-token agreement: a greedy reply is the top token at each position, so this counts how often the proved model makes the original's choice, at every one of the 512 positions, not only up to the first difference.

    Matched leading tokens set the Fidelity check and the Fidelity petal: the tokens at the start of each reply that equal the 16-bit original's, counted up to the first difference. The Fidelity check passes when the replies the system proved match more of them than the bar, a damaged copy, by a paired test at p < 0.05. The Fidelity figure sets no state. Neither compares logits: the KL columns do.
