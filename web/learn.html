    Learn

    A proof of inference lets anyone check, without the model's weights, that a reply is what a model, committed in advance, produces for its prompt.

    For policy readersNo cryptography needed.2 pagesFor prover teams and engineers3 pages

    For policy readers

    1. 01What a proof showsA proof shows a reply is the committed model's greedy reply to its prompt, not whose model it is or that the reply was served.
    2. 02Deployments and treatiesProving costs a large multiple of serving, and checking needs no weights or trusted chip. A treaty could prove replies sampled from a trusted record.

    For prover teams and engineers

    1. 01How we compareEvery system gets the same request. Those passing the six checks, with a stated security level, are ranked by cost vs serving inside one model.
    2. 02The statementThe systems on the ladder prove a committed, quantized copy's greedy reply. They differ in what one proof covers and what their error bound covers.
    3. 03Proving a forward passThe verifier tests the prover's committed values at random points. Matrix products with the committed weights are the cheap part to prove.
